The Floor runs on iPad. Above a certain width the list stays beside whatever you have open, so opening a channel or a profile no longer takes the list off the screen, and the window takes all four orientations and shares the screen with another app.

Home holds both lists. Channels and Contacts are a switch on one screen, under the title, Settings and the channel you are present in, which stay pinned while the list scrolls. Start a channel and Add a contact are the first row of their list, and contact requests are on Contacts.

Close and Settings are drawn as a cross and a gear, in every header that has them.

You can choose a username on your profile — five characters or more, letters, digits and underscores — and Contacts then offers Copy Invite Link. The link seats one person: the first to open it becomes your contact outright, and it lasts thirty days. Invitations sent to an email address carry it too.

Transcripts and watch parties are behind a new setting, "Show experimental features", under Labs in Settings, and it is off to begin with. Until you turn it on, Transcribe is not on a recording and "Watch something together" is not in a channel.

Two settings have new names, each naming what turning it on does. "Tap a channel to step in" is now "Tap a channel to look, not step in", and "Repeat the channel controls as cards" is now "Hide the repeated channel controls". What you had chosen is kept.

The Headphones setting "Keep the connection steady" is gone, and headphones now hold the speaking connection whenever there is audio in the channel, for everybody.

Stepping into a quiet channel leaves whatever else you are playing running. If something else is playing while you would be waiting alone in a channel, you are stepped out to Nearby rather than held there present.

A channel where nobody is speaking and nothing is playing steps everybody out after fifteen minutes, so it stops reading as occupied to the people who are not in it. In a browser, a tab nobody has touched for fifteen minutes steps itself out.

Having the app open counts as attention for as long as it is open, so reading a channel keeps you present in it.

Somebody stepping in and speaking is audible while your phone is locked and the app is in the background.
