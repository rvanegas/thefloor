# The introduction sent to users

**Standing.** The text sent by email to somebody who has just joined, and the
thing to point an existing user at when they ask how something works. Not
temporary: it is a deliverable rather than a design, and it is revised as the
app changes rather than deleted when something ships.

**Derived from PROPOSITION.md** on 2026-09-03, with LISTING.md and ROADMAP.md.
The listing has to fit in a store field and be repeatable by a recommender;
this has neither constraint and one obligation the listing does not have — **it
is the only place the habit gets taught.** The proposition names an observed
failure that is not a bug in anything: somebody pings and then puts the phone
away as though a call were coming. Nothing in the app currently says otherwise,
and until it does (ROADMAP.md § *Teach the habit at the empty channel*), this
email is the correction.

**Vocabulary is GLOSSARY.md's**, Part One only. Every noun below — *channel*,
*present*, *nearby*, *ping*, *floor*, *guest*, *seat* — is used in the narrow
sense that file gives it, because the app's own screens use them that way and a
manual that drifts teaches somebody to misread the interface.

**Two things this must never become.** It is not a feature tour, and it is not
sent twice. An app whose thesis is non-interruption cannot mail you to remind
you it exists; this goes out once, on joining, and otherwise only when a person
asks for it.

## Subject

> The Floor — how it works, and the one habit it needs

## The whole thing in four lines

For people who read four lines and no more, which is most people.

> The Floor is a group chat, except you talk. A channel is a place you drop
> into rather than a call you answer, and **nothing about it rings** — when
> somebody wants you, you get an ordinary notification and you come when you
> can.
>
> **The one habit:** after you ask somebody to come, stay reachable. What is
> coming back is a banner, not an alarm, and a banner cannot fetch you from
> another room. You do not have to sit and stare at the app — leaving it is
> fine, and it will not stop your music. Just stay somewhere a notification
> reaches you.

## The body

### What this is

The Floor is for talking with people you already know. It is built on one
observation: **the ring was designed for emergencies and then used for
everything.** It demands your attention in the next four seconds regardless of
what the call is about, which is why most people have switched it off, and why
we all text instead — not because typing is better than talking, but because a
message waits and a call does not.

So this is voice with a waiting state. **Nothing here rings, ever.** The app
does not present itself to your phone as a telephone call, and it never claims
the notification levels that break through a Focus mode or the silent switch.
When somebody wants you, a banner arrives like any other. You come when you
can, or you don't, and nobody is owed an explanation either way.

### Channels, and why they are not calls

**A channel is a place, not a call.** It holds up to six people, keeps its name
and its recordings between conversations, and is still there tomorrow whether
or not anybody is in it. You do not start one and end one; you **step in** and
**step out**, and the channel carries on existing.

Six is not a technical limit. Past about six, with nobody in charge, voices
collide and the conversation stops being a conversation — and these channels
have no administrators by design. Six people who can all see each other are
their own moderators.

You are present in **one channel at a time**, across every device you are
signed in on. One account has one voice.

### How a conversation actually starts

This is the part that is different from a phone, and the part worth reading
twice.

**Look before you say anything.** Your list of channels tells you where
everybody is: which channels have somebody in them right now, and for the rest,
how long ago somebody other than you was last there. Walking into a channel
somebody is already sitting in is the whole ritual — there is nothing to dial
and nobody to answer.

**If it is empty, step in anyway and ping.** A ping is a notification to one
person saying you are there and you want them. You can ping anybody who is your
contact. It is rate-limited, so somebody who has just been pinged cannot be
pinged again immediately — a ping is an invitation, not a way to lean on
somebody.

**Then carry on with what you were doing.** This is the bit people get wrong,
and it is telephony's fault rather than theirs. Stepping into an empty channel
does not take your phone over: **whatever you were listening to keeps
playing**, because there is nobody there to hear you yet, and the app only
takes the microphone and the audio when somebody actually arrives. Put the
podcast back on. Answer the email. The channel is a door left open behind you.

**What it asks is that you stay reachable.** Leaving the app is expected — you
are meant to get on with your day, and an app that demanded you stare at a
screen would have reinvented the ring one step further along. What breaks it is
going where a banner cannot find you: the phone face down in another room, the
notifications silenced, the person who came when you asked now unable to tell
you they are here.

The roster says this out loud, which is why it has two different words for
somebody who is not in the channel. **Stepped out** means they left on purpose.
**Nearby** means their connection ran out rather than being given up — as far
as anybody knows they are still within reach and one notification away. When
you see *nearby*, ping them rather than giving up.

### Taking the floor

**Conversation is open. Everybody can speak, all the time.** That is the normal
state and it is what the app sounds like most of the time.

**The floor is for when that is not enough.** Claim it and every other
microphone goes quiet for up to a minute so you can finish a thought; release
it and they all come back. One person holds it at a time, and after a claim
ends there is a short wait before that same person may claim again, so nobody
can hold the floor by tapping fastest.

It is enforced on the audio itself, not asked of people politely — a silenced
person's voice does not reach anybody, whatever their screen says.

**It is not a mute.** Your own **self-mute** is a decision about your own
microphone, it costs you nothing, and the two never overwrite each other.

### Contacts, and the absence of strangers

**Nobody can reach you unless you have both agreed.** You send a contact
request to somebody's email address, or to somebody you have met in a channel,
and nothing happens until they accept. There is no directory, no search for
strangers, and no way to be added to anything without saying yes. Becoming
somebody's contact comes with a channel for the two of you.

Being in the same channel as somebody is not the same as being their contact —
channels hold people a mutual friend brought in, and inviting and pinging check
the two separately.

### Bringing somebody in who is not on the app

**Share a guest link.** Whoever follows it opens the channel in a browser, with
no account and nothing to install. They **knock**, somebody already in the room
opens the door, and they are in.

A guest can listen, and can speak only if a member turns their microphone on.
They cannot record, cannot claim the floor, and cannot reach anything else of
yours. The link is live only while people are actually in the channel — it
cannot be sent ahead of time, and it stops working once the room is empty.

### Recording, and the things around it

**Recording only ever happens because somebody in the channel started one**,
and it is announced continuously to everybody in the room, guests included.
Ordinary conversation is not recorded.

Every voice is captured on its own track, so what comes back is clear rather
than a scramble. A recording belongs to the **channel**, not to whoever started
it — anybody in the channel can play it, rename it, export it or delete it, and
it carries the same name for everybody. Deleting a channel deletes its
recordings.

**A transcript** can be made from a recording on request. It goes to a
third-party provider, named on the screen that asks; asking sends everybody's
audio out, so who asked is always shown. Everybody gets one free.

**A watch party** is a YouTube link everybody watches in step on their own
screens — nothing is fetched, stored or recorded here. **Mute the room**
withholds everybody's microphone while the video plays and gives them all back
when you pause it, because you pause a film to talk about it.

**The clipboard** is one piece of text the channel holds, which anybody in it
can read, replace or clear. It is silent, so the floor does not govern it.

### Notifications, and why we are asking for the permission

**Please turn them on.** Here is the argument rather than the request, because
declining notifications from an app you just installed is a good habit and we
would rather you kept it.

Everything this app does travels as a notification. There is no ring and no
call screen — that is the entire point — so an invitation, a knock and a ping
are all ordinary banners. **A banner that was never delivered is an invitation
nobody declined.** Without the permission the app does not merely get less
convenient; the thing it is for stops working, and it fails quietly: somebody
pings you, sees the ping go through, and waits for a person who will never
learn they were wanted.

What we will not do with it, and each of these is structural rather than a
promise about our restraint:

- **Every notification is sent by a person, not by the app.** A named human
  wanted you, right then.
- **Nothing sounds unless you asked it to.** Whether an arrival makes a noise
  is a property of you, the person receiving it — never something a sender can
  choose. Nobody here can flag their call urgent, because every urgent flag in
  the history of messaging has been overclaimed until it meant nothing.
- **There is no re-engagement traffic of any kind.** No streaks, no digests, no
  reminder that a channel misses you. An app whose whole argument is
  non-interruption cannot notify you to say it exists.

If you have already declined, iOS remembers — turn it back on in the Settings
app under The Floor. And it is worth checking on the people you talk to: if
somebody never seems to answer a ping, their notifications may simply be off.

### Signing in

There is no password. You give an email address, a six-digit code arrives, and
typing it signs you in. Codes expire after ten minutes and asking for another
is free; if it does not arrive, check spam first. Signing in somewhere new
leaves your other devices signed in — a phone and a browser can both stay
signed in at once — and **Sign out other devices** in Settings ends every
session but the one you are using.

### Getting help, and getting out

**Write to the support address on the listing.** It is read by a person, and
there is no queue, no ticket number and no bot. Say what happened, what you
expected, and roughly when, if it was about a particular conversation.

**Deleting your account** is in Settings, at the bottom of the Account section,
and it takes effect immediately. Your address, your name, what you wrote about
yourself and your contacts go. Channels you share with other people carry on
without you and keep their recordings — those are other people's copies of a
conversation they were in. Channels you were the only member of go with
everything in them.

### What this app will not do

Not a roadmap, and not modesty. These are the constraints the app is built
against, and the reason to state them to you is that they are what you are
being asked to rely on.

- **No alarm a sender can choose.** If loudness ever arrives, it will be
  something you grant to one named person, and it will be scarce by
  construction rather than by etiquette.
- **No notification that is not from a person.** Ever.
- **No strangers and no directory.** Reach is by mutual invitation. Growth that
  needed us to relax this would be growth that dismantled the thing.
- **Not a messenger.** Text lives in the threads you already have; this app
  supplies the room. Your group chat decides *when*, and this decides *where*.
- **No administrators inside a channel.** Six peers and a door.
- **No advertising and no analytics.** There is a donation link in Settings; it
  is optional and unlocks nothing.

### Yes, keep your phone

The dialer is still installed and emergencies are still emergencies. **Normal
usage should not be patterned after the rare case** — that is the mistake this
app exists to correct. The Floor takes the other ninety-five percent of the
reasons you would call somebody.
