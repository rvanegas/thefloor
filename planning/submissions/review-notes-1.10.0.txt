1. SCREEN RECORDING AND DEVICES
None attached; section 3 walks it. No purchases, no tracking, no ATT prompt.
iPhone is portrait and turns sideways only for a film; iPad takes all four.

2. WHAT IT DOES
Voice conversation between people who know each other. A channel holds six
members and outlasts a conversation; anyone present may claim "the floor",
withholding every other microphone for a minute.

3. GETTING IN AND REACHING FEATURES
No passwords; a code is emailed. This account has a fixed
one: appreview@rvanegas.co / SIX-DIGIT CODE GOES HERE BEFORE SUBMITTING

It holds a contact, Sam Rivera, and three channels; "Weekly Convo" has
recordings.

Home switches between Channels, Contacts, Podcasts and Support. Open "Weekly
Convo". The bottom bar is Mute, Claim and the rungs In, Nearby and Out.
A member's profile has "Mute them", Ping and "Move to remove them".

The tabs across the top: People, Clipboard, Invite (a contact as "Guest" or
"Member", or "Share a guest link"), Record, Listen and Watch - copy a YouTube
link, then Paste. "Start a channel", on Home, makes one holding only you; its
Settings offer "Make channel into a community" and "This channel is a podcast".

NEW: Record replaces Recordings: Audio records, Pause holds it, and the log
lists what was kept a room at a time. Transcribe is on a recording's row.

NEW, AND WHY UIBackgroundModes HAS voip: stepping into a channel is reported
to CallKit as an outgoing call, so it shows in Recents and other calls meet
it as a call. There is no PushKit and nothing ever rings.

Floor Settings, from Home: Language, "Choose where sound comes out", Email.

NOT BUGS: a channel holds you on one device at a time; "Claim the floor" is
unavailable while alone; Ping is offered only to somebody in the channel or
nearby it; Audio off is final until the room empties; Record's Text switch,
the live transcript, is ours to pay for and switch, so it is grey; the room is muted while a
film plays on the device you stepped in on; a podcast needs a named channel;
removing a member needs a second member to agree, except a community's owner.

4. EXTERNAL SERVICES
LiveKit (open-source, self-hosted) for audio and recordings; S3 stores them and
cover art; SES for sign-in codes; APNs; Ko-fi; YouTube's IFrame player, only
once a member starts a watch party; AssemblyAI, sent a recording somebody asks
to transcribe, or a channel's audio while its live transcript is on.

5. REGIONAL DIFFERENCES
The Ko-fi link on Support shows only in the US storefront (3.1.1(a));
voluntary, unlocks nothing, and the app is free.

6. THIRD-PARTY MATERIAL, PUBLISHING AND WATCH PARTIES
A member may play their own audio file in. A watch party sends only a position
and a clock; YouTube's own IFrame player plays the video, unmodified.

A podcast is a public page and RSS feed. A conversation reaches it only when
every account heard in it agrees; any one withdrawing takes it down at once.
No member is named, and both carry a takedown address read by a person.

GUIDELINE 1.2: what can be found is channels that opted in, never people. A
username names its owner's invite link, which only they hand out. A field
taking an email address sends a request there and returns nothing. A contact
needs both sides' consent; only contacts see each other's email and phone,
and Ping is contacts-only.

Two exceptions. A community admits anybody its owner gives the link to, up to
twenty, making nobody a contact; its page names no member, and the owner alone
invites, plays, records, removes and deletes. And signing up without an
invitation and knowing nobody puts you, once, into "Getting Started Cohort"
with up to three others who joined then and one of us; leaving is a tap.

Guests: a visitor with a link knocks under a typed name and a member admits
them; up to forty, two with a microphone, all gone when the last member
leaves. Recording and
transcribing show to guests.
