Watch something together. Paste a YouTube link into a channel and everyone gets the same clock: play, pause and seek reach all of you at once. The phone is the remote. The video plays on a page you open on a laptop or a tablet, in YouTube's own player, and it follows the room by itself. Whoever holds the floor decides what plays. You can mute the room for the film and unmute by pausing it.

Recordings can be transcribed. Ask for one and the text arrives beside the recording, split by who was speaking, with a way to name the voices and to say that two of them are the same person. Search a channel for a word and it finds the moment. Whatever was played into the room is transcribed too, on its own track, so a voice cannot hide the music. Your first transcript is free.

Tap a contact and you get their profile: every channel you share, when they were last in each one, and a way to go straight there. You can hand them your email address, and copy theirs if they have handed you theirs.

You can be signed in on more than one device now. Your voice is in one place at a time, and stepping into a channel on the phone steps you out of it on the tablet. Notifications no longer buzz the device you are already talking on. Settings has "Sign out other devices" for the one you left behind.

Turning notifications on from iOS Settings is noticed as soon as you come back to the app, rather than at the next launch.

Removing a contact is a quiet row at the foot of their card rather than a red button in the middle of it.
