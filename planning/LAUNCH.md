# The launch surfaces, and what to say on each

**Temporary**, in the sense DESCRIPTION.md and LISTING.md are temporary: this
is copy argued before it is posted. Delete it once the posts are made, moving
whatever the comments actually taught into `decisions/`.

**Written 2026-09-14**, as the fourth deliverable of the marketing work.
planning/MARKETING.md is the argument this file executes — read § *Organic,
ranked* item 3 before changing anything here, since it says what these
surfaces are *for*, which is not what they appear to be for.

**The one thing to hold on to.** Their value is disproportionately in the
**comments**, not the post. A launch post is read once; a good comment thread
where the thesis is argued rather than asserted is what gets quoted afterwards
and what ranks in search for years. So § *The objections* below is the longest
section in this file on purpose, and it is the part to actually prepare.

**And the post is the thesis, never the feature list.** This app loses a
feature comparison to Discord, Zello and the platform walkie-talkies, all of
which have more of everything. It wins the argument about what the ring is for.
Lead with the argument.

## Before any of it

Two blockers, both from MARKETING.md § *The sequence*:

1. **Screenshots.** There are none in the repository; the store's live in App
   Store Connect and nowhere else. **Product Hunt without imagery is a post
   that was not made**, and Reddit is nearly as bad. Show HN is the exception
   and is why it is first here.
2. **`bin/growth`.** Post without it and the day produces a number nobody can
   interpret and no way to tell a good community from a bad one. Not a hard
   blocker; a strong preference.

**Post to one surface at a time**, for the same reason MARKETING.md gives for
sequencing paid: the measurement is thin enough that a simultaneous launch
cannot be read afterwards.

## Show HN

**First, because it is the one surface where the argument is the product and
the absence of screenshots costs nothing.** The audience is also unusually
likely to contain the exact user described in PROPOSITION.md — somebody who
turned the ringer off years ago and has opinions about why.

### Title

Under 80 characters, no marketing voice, no exclamation. Three, best first:

> Show HN: The Floor – group voice that never rings you

> Show HN: A voice app with a waiting state

> Show HN: Voice chat for people who find phone calls rude

**The first is the recommended one.** It categorises in three words and states
the differentiator in four, which is what a title on that site has to do. The
second is the most interesting and the least legible — it is the thesis in five
words but nobody scanning knows what it is a category of. The third tests
better with the intended audience and slightly worse with everybody else.

### Body

Posted as the text of the submission, or as the first comment if the
submission is a link. Written in the first person, because that site treats
the third person as a press release.

> The telephone ring is an affordance built for the rare case and applied to
> all traffic. It demands attention in the next four seconds regardless of what
> the call is about, and the phone has spent a century building defenses
> against itself — busy signal, call waiting, a hardware mute switch, Do Not
> Disturb, Focus modes. Those aren't six inventions, they're six patches on one
> category error. Most people have simply switched the ringer off.
>
> Texting won on intrusiveness, not convenience. But what the ring actually
> buys isn't voice, it's synchrony, and texting proved those were separable.
> Which names the missing thing: voice has no "later." Answer or decline, green
> or red, no third option. Every text has a waiting state built in and no phone
> call ever has.
>
> The Floor is an attempt at giving voice a waiting state. A channel is a place
> rather than a call — up to six people, it keeps its name between
> conversations, and you drop in when it suits you. When somebody wants you,
> you get an ordinary notification that waits in line with everything else.
> There's no CallKit and no VoIP push in it at all; it never claims
> time-sensitive or critical, so it can't pierce a Focus mode even if I wanted
> it to.
>
> The hard part isn't the audio, it's the empty room. A message survives the
> recipient's absence and a room doesn't. The answer so far is presence and a
> ping: the list shows who's in which channel now and how long ago somebody was
> last in the others, and if it's empty you step in, ping, and put the phone
> down — stepping in doesn't take the device over, so whatever you were playing
> keeps playing, and when they arrive you can talk with the screen off. Whether
> that's enough without a text thread doing the coordinating is the open
> question I'd most like opinions on.
>
> One other thing that turned out to matter: conversation is open by default,
> and "the floor" is a claim somebody makes when they need to finish a thought
> — every other mic goes quiet until they give it back, enforced on the audio
> rather than asked for politely. It's the same principle as the ring, one
> level in: overtalking is intrusion, and the fix is a boundary rather than a
> moderator.
>
> iOS, and there's a browser client. It's free, no ads, no analytics. Contact
> is mutual and by invitation — no directory, no search for strangers — which
> is also why it grows slowly, and I'd rather that than the alternative.

**Why it ends on the constraint.** That site rewards a post that names its own
limitation before a commenter does, and the slow-growth admission pre-empts
the most common objection in § *The objections*.

**The question in the fourth paragraph is deliberate and is the real reason to
post.** MARKETING.md § *The funnel* names level 9 as one of the three leaky
ones, and ROADMAP item 3 is *settle whether ping is enough on its own*. Asking
it in public is cheaper than any other way of finding out.

## Product Hunt

**Second, and only once screenshots exist.** That audience is builders and
early adopters — closer to the app's user than a general audience, further than
Show HN's — and the format is image-first in a way the others are not.

### Tagline (60 characters)

> Group voice that waits for you. Nothing ever rings.

Fifty-one characters. **It is the listing's subtitle plus the pillar**, and the
second sentence is the negation that LISTING.md § *Saying it forwards* would
normally turn around. It is kept here and not there because a tagline gets one
line and no second chance, and on this surface the reader is scanning past
forty other products — the pain has to be named, not implied.

### Description

> The Floor is for talking with people you already know. A channel is a place
> you drop into rather than a call you answer: up to six people, it keeps its
> name between conversations, and whoever is there is there.
>
> When somebody wants you, you get an ordinary notification — it waits in line
> with all the others, your ringer stays yours, and your Focus mode holds.
> There's no CallKit, no VoIP push, and nothing that can claim the escalations
> that pierce a silent switch.
>
> Conversation is open. When one person needs to be heard properly they take
> the floor, and every other microphone stays quiet until they give it back.
>
> Everybody in it is somebody you both agreed to. No directory, no strangers,
> no feed.

### The maker's first comment

**The most-read text on that site after the tagline**, and the place the
thesis goes.

> I built this because I'd had my ringer off for about six years and eventually
> noticed that's not a preference, it's a verdict. The ring is an alarm, and
> it's the default for every call regardless of what the call is about — a
> thing built for the rare case, applied to all traffic.
>
> Texting won because it doesn't seize you. But the ring was never buying
> voice, it was buying synchrony, and text proved those come apart. So voice is
> the one medium left with no "later": answer or decline, and nothing in
> between. This is an attempt at the third option.
>
> The design constraint I'd point at, because it's the one that shaped
> everything: **a sender can never declare urgency here.** Whether an arrival
> makes a sound is a property of the recipient, not a mode the caller picks.
> Every urgent flag in the history of messaging has been overclaimed by senders
> until it meant nothing, so if this ever gains an alarm it has to arrive as a
> permission you grant a specific person.
>
> Happy to talk about any of it — especially the empty-room problem, which is
> the real objection and the thing I'm least sure I've solved.

## Reddit

**Third, highest risk, highest density.** These are the places where whole
groups already talk to themselves, which is the unit MARKETING.md says is the
only one worth acquiring. They are also the places most likely to ban an
account for what looks like a launch.

**The rule that gets you banned from all of them: arriving as a launch.** Read
each community's self-promotion rule, be a participant first, and post the
thesis rather than the product. A comment answering somebody's actual problem
with *I built a thing that does this, here's why the design is what it is* is
worth more than any submission and is allowed nearly everywhere that a
submission is not.

Three shapes, in descending order of fit:

**1. The complaint communities** — where people are already saying they hate
phone calls, keep the ringer off, and answer nothing. The post is the thesis
with no product in the title, and the app appears in the body as *what I
ended up building*, once.

> I've had my ringer off for years and it finally occurred to me that's not a
> preference, it's a verdict on the feature. The ring is an alarm built for
> emergencies and applied to every call — and every defense the phone has
> grown since is a patch on that. Call waiting, DND, Focus, the hardware mute
> switch. Six patches, one mistake.
>
> What I can't get past is that text has a waiting state and voice doesn't.
> Answer or decline, and nothing in between. Am I wrong that this is the whole
> problem? [then, and only if the rules allow: one sentence naming the app]

**2. The group communities** — book clubs, tabletop, amateur music, running
clubs, long-distance families, co-op gaming. **Never a launch post.** The
opening is a coordination problem they already have: the thread where everybody
agrees on a time and then nobody calls. The app is offered as an answer to that
specific problem, in a comment, when somebody raises it.

**3. The maker communities**, where a launch post is the format and the
audience is the same one Show HN reaches. Reuse the Show HN body.

**What to do in every one of them afterwards:** answer the objections below,
in public, at length, without defensiveness. That is the whole return on the
day.

## The objections

**Eleven, and four of them are correct.** Say so when they are — a launch
thread where the maker concedes the true objections is the one that gets
believed about everything else.

**1. "This is just Discord."** Nearest true thing said. The difference is not
features, it is who is in it: Discord is a server you join and this has no
directory, no search and no strangers — everybody in it agreed twice. And the
floor is enforced on the audio rather than being a moderator's job, because
there are no moderators here by design. *Never name Discord in store copy*
(LISTING.md: 4.1 exposure); naming it in a comment thread where somebody else
raised it is fine and expected.

**2. "This is just a walkie-talkie app / Zello / push-to-talk."** Those are
half-duplex by design: you press, you talk, they hear it later. This is an open
conversation that happens to have a boundary available in it. The floor is a
claim for finishing a thought, not the standing mode of operation — and if the
copy anywhere implies otherwise, the copy is wrong.

**3. "Six people is too few."** It is a moderation argument, not a capacity
one: past about six, absent a moderator, voices collide and the conversation
degrades. These channels have no administration by design, so the cap is
standing in for the administrator. Guests extend the listening side without
extending the six.

**4. "No Android." Correct, and concede it immediately.** Partly built — audio
session, foreground service and push are done; the rest is not, and there is no
date. Do not promise one.

**5. "How do I get my friends on it?"** The best objection in the list and the
one the whole product wrestles with. The honest answer: an invite link that
seats exactly one person and makes you contacts outright, so the person you
send it to is not alone when they arrive. And it is genuinely harder than it is
for a messenger, because the thing being asked for is three people rather than
one.

**6. "The empty room. Nobody will ever be there." Correct, and it is the real
objection.** PROPOSITION.md calls it that in those words. The answer is
presence, recency and the ping — and the further answer is that waiting costs
nothing, since stepping into a quiet channel does not take the device over.
**Whether that is enough without a text thread doing the coordinating is
genuinely open.** Say that; it is the question worth asking a crowd.

**7. "Why not just use the phone / FaceTime / a group call?"** Because all of
them ring, which means all of them require the recipient's *now*. That is the
whole argument and it is fine to just restate it.

**8. "So it's a call I might miss."** It is a call nobody has to decline. The
invitation waits, the way a text waits. What is lost is the guarantee of
immediate attention, which is exactly the thing being given up on purpose.

**9. "No analytics, really?"** True as of this writing and `/privacy` says so.
**If first-party counting has shipped by the time this is posted, update this
answer rather than repeating the old one** — MARKETING.md § *Measurement* is
the current state, and a stale privacy claim repeated in a launch thread is
worse than never having made it.

**10. "What's the business model?" Concede that there isn't much of one.** It
is free, there is no advertising, there is a donation link, and the only thing
measured is the minutes the server carried so that it can be paid for. Do not
invent a roadmap to revenue in a comment thread.

**11. "Recording — so you keep everything?"** No: a conversation passes through
and is gone, and what is kept is what somebody in the channel chose to record.
Every voice on its own track, named once for everybody, and anyone in the
channel can delete it rather than only whoever started it. Point at `/privacy`,
which is specific and more convincing than any paraphrase.

## What not to do

From MARKETING.md § *What marketing may not do*, in the forms that will
specifically tempt somebody on launch day:

- **No "invite 3 friends and get X."** No incentivised referral, ever. It
  optimises for the unit that evaporates and corrupts the one honest signal in
  the system.
- **No launch-day push notification** to existing users, and no email of that
  shape. There is no re-engagement traffic here, and a launch is not an
  exception to it — it is exactly the kind of occasion that makes people think
  it should be.
- **No comparison table.** Naming a competitor in the store is a 4.1 exposure,
  and putting the app on a feature grid is fighting on the ground it loses on.
- **No waitlist, no scarcity, no "launching soon."** The app is in the store.
- **No claim that is not in the shipped build.** Open channels and
  alarm-by-permission are arguments, not features, and a launch thread is where
  somebody will say them out loud for the first time.
